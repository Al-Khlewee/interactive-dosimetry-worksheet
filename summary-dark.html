Reference Dosimetry, Explained

How a small electric charge from an ion chamber becomes a trusted absorbed dose, in one page.

01 The big idea

An ionization chamber is a small pocket of air. Radiation knocks electrons off the air molecules and the electrometer collects that charge as a reading, M, in nanocoulombs.

Charge is not dose. A standards lab has measured how many grays per nanocoulomb your chamber gives in cobalt-60: the calibration coefficient ND,w,Qo.

You measure under standard conditions, correct the reading for today's air and chamber behaviour, and convert it to your accelerator's beam.

Dw,Q = MQ × ND,w,Qo × kQ,Qo

02 The measurement setup

Photons: zref = 10 g/cm², SSD or SAD 100 cm.
Electrons: zref = 0.6 R50 − 0.1 g/cm².

05 Photons vs electrons

Photon beams

Quality: TPR20,10
Depth: 10 g/cm²
Field: 10 × 10 cm
Chamber: cylindrical

Electron beams

Quality: R50
Depth: 0.6 R50 − 0.1 g/cm²
Field: ≥ 10 × 10 cm²
Chamber: plane-parallel

06 Key takeaways

  1. Standard conditions make every calibration comparable.
  2. Every factor should be close to 1. A large one points to a setup or equipment problem.
  3. Traceability runs from your result back to a primary standard.
  4. Record everything: the worksheet is your audit trail.
