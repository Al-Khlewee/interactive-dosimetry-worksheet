Dosimetry as a Handbook

Reference dosimetry is a repeatable recipe: the same conditions, the same measurements, the same corrections, every time. Treat it like a handbook and every beam calibration becomes traceable, comparable and auditable.

Dw,Q = MQ × ND,w,Qo × kQ,Qo

Absorbed dose to water = corrected reading × calibration coefficient × beam-quality factor

Why use dosimetry like a handbook?

📘

One standard procedure

Fixed reference conditions and a fixed order of steps mean any physicist gets the same answer on the same beam.

🔗

Traceable to a standard

Every result links back through the chamber's calibration certificate to a primary standards laboratory.

🗂️

A record you can look up

Each worksheet captures inputs, factors and dose, so past calibrations can be compared, audited and trended.

The handbook recipe: six steps

  1. 1

    Set reference conditions

    Water phantom, reference field size, SSD or SAD and depth exactly as the code of practice specifies (see the table below).

  2. 2

    Specify the beam quality, Q

    Photons: measure TPR20,10. Electrons: measure R50, then set the depth zref = 0.6 R50 − 0.1 g/cm².

  3. 3

    Prepare the dosimeter

    Note chamber and electrometer models and serial numbers, the calibration coefficient ND,w,Qo, and the lab's reference T0 and P0. Warm up and pre-irradiate.

  4. 4

    Take the readings

    Record T and P, the reading M1 at your normal voltage V1, readings at both polarities (M+, M−) and a reading M2 at a lower voltage V2.

  5. 5

    Apply the corrections

    MQ = M1 × kTP × kelec × kpol × ks, then look up kQ,Qo for your chamber at beam quality Q.

  6. 6

    Calculate, compare, record

    Compute Dw,Q (Gy/MU) and dose rate, compare with the last calibration and the expected output, then print and file the report.

Correction factors at a glance

Air density, kTP

Corrects for: T & P
kTP = 
273.2 + T273.2 + T0
×
P0P

The chamber is open to air; this normalises the mass of air in the cavity back to the calibration conditions.

Polarity, kpol

Corrects for: polarity effect
kpol = 
|M+| + |M−|2 M

M is the reading at your usual polarity. Expect a value very close to 1; a larger deviation is worth investigating.

Recombination, ks

Corrects for: lost ions
ks = a0 + a1(M1/M2) + a2(M1/M2)2

Two-voltage method for pulsed beams. The coefficients ai come from the tabulated voltage ratio V1/V2.

Electrometer, kelec

Corrects for: electrometer calibration
kelec = from certificate

Equals 1.000 when the chamber and electrometer were calibrated together as one system.

Beam quality, kQ,Qo

Corrects for: beam ≠ calibration beam
kQ,Qo = f(chamber model, TPR20,10 or R50)

Looked up from the code-of-practice tables for your chamber type. It converts the 60Co calibration coefficient to your linac's beam quality.

Handbook checklist before you sign off

  • ✓Calibration certificate is in date and matches the serial numbers.
  • ✓Thermometer has reached equilibrium with the water.
  • ✓Readings are stable and repeated, not single shots.
  • ✓Every correction factor is close to 1; question any outlier.
  • ✓kQ,Qo uses the right chamber model and beam quality.
  • ✓Result agrees with the previous calibration and expected output.
  • ✓Report is printed, signed and filed with the date and user.
  • ✓Any deviation is investigated before clinical use.
