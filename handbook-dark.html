01 Why use dosimetry like a handbook?

One standard procedure

Fixed reference conditions and a fixed order of steps mean any physicist gets the same answer on the same beam.

Traceable to a standard

Every result links back through the chamber's calibration certificate to a primary standards laboratory.

A record you can look up

Each worksheet captures inputs, factors and dose, so past calibrations can be compared, audited and trended.

03 The handbook recipe: six steps

1

Set reference conditions

Water phantom, reference field size, SSD or SAD and depth exactly as the code of practice specifies (page 3).

2

Specify the beam quality, Q

Photons: measure TPR20,10. Electrons: measure R50, then set zref = 0.6 R50 − 0.1 g/cm².

3

Prepare the dosimeter

Record chamber and electrometer models and serial numbers, ND,w,Qo, and the lab's T0 and P0. Warm up and pre-irradiate.

4

Take the readings

Record T and P, M1 at your normal voltage V1, readings at both polarities (M+, M−) and M2 at a lower voltage V2.

5

Apply the corrections

MQ = M1 × kTP × kelec × kpol × ks, then look up kQ,Qo for your chamber at beam quality Q.

6

Calculate, compare, record

Compute Dw,Q (Gy/MU) and dose rate, compare with the last calibration and expected output, then sign and file the report.

04 Correction factors at a glance

Air density, kTP

T & P
kTP =273.2 + T273.2 + T0×P0P

The chamber is open to air; this normalises the mass of air in the cavity back to calibration conditions.

Polarity, kpol

Polarity effect
kpol =|M+| + |M−|2 M

M is the reading at your usual polarity. Expect a value very close to 1; a larger deviation is worth investigating.

Recombination, ks

Lost ions
ks = a0 + a1(M1/M2) + a2(M1/M2)2

Two-voltage method for pulsed beams. The coefficients ai come from the tabulated voltage ratio V1/V2.

Electrometer, kelec

Electrometer cal.
kelec = from certificate

Equals 1.000 when the chamber and electrometer were calibrated together as one system.

Beam quality, kQ,Qo

Beam ≠ calibration beam
kQ,Qo = f(chamber model, TPR20,10 or R50)

Looked up from the code-of-practice tables for your chamber type. It converts the 60Co calibration coefficient to your accelerator's beam quality.

06 Checklist before you sign off

  • Calibration certificate is in date and matches the serial numbers.
  • Thermometer has reached equilibrium with the water.
  • Readings are stable and repeated, not single shots.
  • Every correction factor is close to 1; question any outlier.
  • kQ,Qo uses the right chamber model and beam quality.
  • Result agrees with the previous calibration and expected output.
  • Report is printed, signed and filed with the date and user.
  • Any deviation is investigated before clinical use.
